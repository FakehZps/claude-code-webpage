## Two Games, One Cart

The pitch still sounds absurd on paper — take Tetris, take Puyo Puyo, and let them fight in the same modes, the same lobbies, sometimes the same board. It shouldn't work. It works. Sega's puzzle-crossover format is somehow more coherent the second time around, with modes that actually let squares and blobs collide instead of just coexisting.

> LINE CLEAR: Squares eliminated. Blobs unaffected. Chain: broken.

## Swap Mode

The new Swap format is the best addition — boards alternate between Tetris and Puyo mid-match, forcing you to context-switch your entire mental model on the fly. It's chaotic in the best way and exposes how differently the two games ask you to think.

> CHAIN CONFIRMED: 4x. Opponent's board: buried.

## The Grind

There's not a lot of narrative ambition here — a light story mode exists mostly as an excuse to unlock characters — but the core competitive loop is tight enough that it doesn't need one. This is a couch-multiplayer machine first and foremost.

## Final Verdict

*Squares and blobs.* An uncomplicated, mechanically excellent puzzle crossover that has no business working as well as it does.
