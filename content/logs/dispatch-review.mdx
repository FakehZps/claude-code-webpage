## Clocking In

Dispatch drops you behind a desk, not a cape. Robert used to be a superhero. Now he coordinates a team of barely-reformed supervillains through a dispatch terminal, routing them to crimes they are absolutely not qualified to stop. It's a premise that could've been a gimmick. Instead it's the whole engine — every dispatch decision has a consequence, every radio call is a character beat.

> DISPATCH LOG: Unit assigned. Unit is on fire. Unit remains, somehow, enthusiastic.

## The Cast

This is a game that understands the AdHoc pedigree it's carrying. The ensemble is stacked with personalities who could each headline their own show, and the writing gives every one of them a real arc buried in the jokes. The interactive-episode structure — half management sim, half branching comedy — never lets the tone sag.

> RADIO CHATTER: "I'm a beetch. My name's Robert. I'm a beetch whose name is Robert."

That line alone justified the purchase. The game is full of them.

## The Management Layer

The dispatch mechanics are simple by design — you're not meant to min-max your way through crises, you're meant to watch what happens when you send the wrong lunatic to the wrong job. It's light enough to never overshadow the writing, which is clearly the point.

## Final Verdict

*I'm a beetch. My name's Robert.* A comedy game that's actually funny, with a cast worth every minute spent routing them into disaster.
