## Climbing Celeste Mountain

Celeste's pixel-perfect platforming has a reputation, and about two hundred deaths in I understood exactly why. Dashing, wall-jumping, and climbing through screen after screen of spikes and disappearing blocks is unforgiving by design — every death is instant, every respawn is instant, and the game wants you to treat failure as friction, not punishment.

> RESPAWN: instant. Death count: not tracked. Ego: bruised anyway.

## Turning On Assist Mode

I turned on Assist Mode a few chapters in, slowing the game down and granting infinite stamina to get past the sections that were putting a hard stop on my progress. It didn't feel like cheating so much as choosing which parts of the mountain I wanted to fight — the platforming stayed exactly as tight, I just stopped being the bottleneck on some of the meaner gauntlets.

> ASSIST ACTIVE: game speed reduced. Judgment from the game: none given.

## Madeline

What actually stuck with me wasn't the movement tech, it was Madeline's arc — an anxiety narrative told through level design as much as dialogue, where the mountain's difficulty is the metaphor and not just an obstacle course bolted on top of one. The game meeting players wherever they're at with Assist Mode built in, rather than hidden behind a menu of shame, fits that theme perfectly.

## Final Verdict

*Assisted.* A mountain worth climbing however you get up it — Celeste's design is generous enough that leaning on Assist Mode never once dulled the trip to the summit.
