## Hope County

Far Cry 5 swaps the series' usual tropical/mountainous exotica for rural Montana, and the change of scenery does more work than it should. Cornfields, grain silos, and a cult calling itself Eden's Gate dressed in flannel — it's a genuinely unsettling setting precisely because it looks like home.

> RESISTANCE RADIO: Signal acquired. Joseph Seed still preaching. Volume: too loud.

## Joseph Seed

The Father is one of the better Far Cry villains — a cult leader who actually believes his own doctrine, which makes him scarier than a cackling warlord. The problem is the game keeps cutting away from him to reset your progress every time you get close, a structural tic that undercuts the tension it spends so much effort building.

> OUTPOST CLEARED: Eden's Gate presence: reduced. Eden's Gate resolve: unaffected.

## The Collapse

The base game is a solid, familiar tower-and-outpost loop elevated by a genuinely disturbing setting and a strong voice cast. It's the ending — the Collapse — that stays with you. A nihilistic gut-punch that the marketing never prepared anyone for, and one of the few times this franchise has actually committed to a dark idea instead of flinching from it.

## Final Verdict

*The Collapse.* Familiar Far Cry mechanics wrapped around a setting and ending with more teeth than the series usually allows itself.
