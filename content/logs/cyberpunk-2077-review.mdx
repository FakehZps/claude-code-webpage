## Night City

Cyberpunk 2077 is the kind of game that doesn't let go. Picked it up on PS5 years after the launch disaster everyone remembers, and what's left is something else entirely — a dense, vertical, neon-choked city that rewards just wandering its districts as much as it does following the main job. Night City sells the fantasy completely: the corpo towers of City Center, the gang-controlled sprawl of Pacifica, the quiet desperation of Watson. Few open worlds have this much personality baked into every block.

## V and the Relic

Starting as a Nomad shaped the whole playthrough — arriving in Night City as an outsider looking in changes how every faction, every fixer, every back-alley job lands. Johnny Silverhand living rent-free in V's skull is the game's best idea, a rockerboy ghost with strong opinions about everything V does, voiced by Keanu Reeves in a performance that's doing more heavy lifting than it has any right to.

"Nomad Samurai Star" is the shorthand for how this one ended: Nomad start, Silverhand's old band as the connective tissue running through the whole story, and the Star ending riding out of the city with Panam and the Aldecados rather than chasing a cure alone. It's the most hopeful of the game's endings, and it earns that hope.

## The Detail

Everything here fires on all cylinders by the version I played. The gunplay and netrunning systems are tight, the city rewards exploration with side jobs that frequently outshine the main story, and there's a confidence to the design — the fixers, the braindances, the constant hum of Night City's radio stations — that keeps pushing you forward.

## Final Verdict

One of the best in its genre. Cyberpunk 2077 earns its reputation. Play it.
