## Landing on Iki

Sucker Punch built something rare here — an open world that earns its bloat. Tsushima's wind-guided exploration, its haiku pillars, its foxes leading you to shrines you didn't ask to find — none of it feels like padding. The Director's Cut folds in the Iki Island expansion, and it's the better half of the game: tighter, weirder, angrier.

> GHOST TALE: Recorded. Jin Sakai's reputation among the people: legend. Among the samurai: traitor.

## The Ghost Path

Every mechanical choice in this game funnels you away from honorable combat and toward the smoke bombs, the poison darts, the standoffs where you cut a man down before he finishes his sentence. It's a good tension — the game keeps reminding you that Lord Shimura would be ashamed, and you keep not caring, because sneaking a blade into a fortress full of Mongols is simply more fun than dueling them one at a time.

> STANDOFF: Enemy stance read. Katana drawn. One strike.

## The Platinum Grind

Getting the plat here is a genuine tour of everything the game does well — every Mongol camp cleared, every haiku written, every fox den visited. It's the kind of trophy list that doesn't punish you for engaging with the systems, because the systems are the reason you're here.

## Final Verdict

*I have no honor... plat'd.* A gorgeous, mechanically confident samurai epic that knows exactly which fantasy it's selling. Iki Island only sharpens it.
